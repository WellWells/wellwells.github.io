Four-Day Self-guided Tour to Singapore

Four days in Singapore: Chinatown, the Cloud Forest at Gardens by the Bay, Mount Faber, S.E.A. Aquarium, the Botanic Gardens, the Zoo and River Wonders, with suggested time at each and the few stops that were not worth it.

Map and Itinerary

This Singapore trip had a relatively flexible itinerary. Landing at Changi International Airport at 6:41 AM on the first day, we took the MRT straight to the hotel. Most hotels only check you in from the afternoon, but we got the room key that morning and had time to freshen up, which made the rest of the day easy to run.

Here is the main 4-day itinerary, most of it planned before we left. The pace was on the relaxed side.

After freshening up in the morning, we headed to Chinatown for lunch, where a meal ran about TWD 200. The area was busy with shops selling festive goods, though most of it was the same kind of souvenir you find in Taiwan, so it didn't hold our attention. After a quick walk around, we stopped at the nearby Ya Kun Kaya Toast for a proper local afternoon tea.

In the afternoon, we took the MRT to Gardens by the Bay. The grounds look good, and it's worth buying tickets in advance through an online platform. For this visit, we had already booked admission for the OCBC Skyway and the Cloud Forest.

The OCBC Skyway is a skywalk suspended between two Supertrees, measuring approximately 128 meters in length and 22 meters in height. Walking the entire path takes around 15 to 30 minutes. From the skywalk you get a clear view of Marina Bay's iconic landmark, the Marina Bay Sands Hotel, and can look out over the whole garden area.

The Cloud Forest was my favorite part of the entire trip. You walk in and the first thing in front of you is a very tall waterfall, and the interior is kept cool. The exhibit holds a wide variety of vegetation and orchids, with an aerial walkway weaving through the space, so walking it feels like being inside a tropical rainforest.

Exploring Gardens by the Bay at a leisurely pace takes about 2.5 hours. Recommended.

After leaving the Gardens, we walked to Merlion Park, about 45 minutes away, stopping at shops along the route for drinks. At Merlion Park we spent about 30 minutes taking photos, which was enough.

Finally, we took the MRT to Song Fa Bak Kut Teh for dinner. There was about a 30-minute queue, but you can order ahead on your phone. Once seated, the dishes came out within 1–2 minutes. Good meal, and that's where day one ended.

Today's main itinerary included Mount Faber Park, S.E.A. Aquarium, and Singapore Botanic Gardens. After breakfast, we took the MRT to HarbourFront Station for the cable car ride. Overcast skies and light rain took the edge off it. Mount Faber Park is a good spot for hiking, with rich plant life and a quiet setting, and we stayed about 1.5 hours. The park itself is attractive, but the humid, muggy weather cut into the comfort of the visit. The weather turned as we were leaving and it started to drizzle.

Water leaked into the cabin during the cable car ride, so the value was poor. For future visits to Mount Faber, take a bus or walk up instead: it costs less and you get a better look at the scenery on the way.

At noon, we had lunch at Malaysian Food Street, located on the outskirts of Universal Studios and the S.E.A. Aquarium. The food court offered a wide variety of options at affordable prices.

In the afternoon, we visited the S.E.A. Aquarium, which has a huge deep-sea exhibit wall and a wide range of marine species. We spent around 1.5 to 2 hours there. Well worth a visit.

Afterward, we headed to the Singapore Botanic Gardens, one of the best botanical gardens I have visited. It holds an extensive variety of plants, all carefully maintained, with blooms all over the grounds. Entry is also free. We stayed about 1.5 hours, and I'd go back if I get the chance.

In the evening, we took the MRT to Wee Nam Kee Hainanese Chicken Rice. The dishes were good, the staff were friendly, and the prices were reasonable. The restaurant itself was spacious and comfortable, and it suits Chinese diners well. The location pin on Google Maps is slightly off, though, so we spent extra time finding the right spot.

The chicken rice was good quality, but not enough to justify a special trip, given that Taiwan has Hainanese chicken rice at a similar standard.

Day 3 Itinerary

No.DestinationNotes
1★ Singapore Zoo
2★ River WondersAmazon River Quest is optional.
3Little IndiaNot recommended.

The visit to the Singapore Zoo typically takes up half a day. In the morning, we took the MRT and transferred to a bus to reach the zoo, arriving close to midday. After a quick meal, we began exploring the zoo, which required approximately 4.5 hours to fully experience.

After leaving the zoo, we proceeded to visit the River Wonders. Compared to the zoo, the River Wonders is smaller in scale, with an estimated exploration time of 1 to 1.5 hours. The following are the key areas and highlights of the River Wonders:

  1. Giant Panda Forest
  2. Amazon Flooded Forest
  3. Congo River Zone
  4. Mekong River Zone
  5. Flooded Forest Tunnel

The Giant Panda Forest left a particularly strong impression. Compared to panda exhibits in other regions, Singapore's design stays much closer to a natural environment, and it's a lot easier on the eye. The pandas, Kai Kai and Jia Jia, live in an open, natural habitat shared with red pandas, and both the viewing and the signage are well done.

The Amazon River Quest in the park requires an additional fee of SGD 5. Since we visited on a weekday afternoon close to closing time (6:00 PM), the queue was relatively short, allowing us to board quickly. Although it is possible to rejoin the queue for another ride after finishing, the overall experience was quite brief and rushed. The number of animals visible along the route was limited, and they appeared fleetingly, making the viewing experience less engaging.

The value on this one is poor. If you have other things planned later in the day, skip it and save the time and energy.

In the evening, we took the MRT to Little India, but it didn't work out, especially the food. We picked a Sri Lankan restaurant that looked decent on Google; the food was mediocre and the room wasn't what we expected either.

Little India isn't a first choice for sightseeing or a meal. Without a specific reason to go, skip it when planning your itinerary.

On the final day, as there was a flight scheduled, the itinerary was kept relatively relaxed. In the morning, we visited the National Museum of Singapore, which is located near the hotel. It is recommended to allocate around 1.5 hours for the visit. Afterward, we headed to the nearby Fort Canning Park for a leisurely stroll, enjoying its natural and cultural scenery.

In the afternoon, we arrived at the airport around 2:30 PM. There's a wide range of food there, so it works well for lunch. The Jewel Rain Vortex inside Jewel Changi Airport is a spectacle in its own right, and that's where the Singapore trip ended.

Conclusion

Planning a trip to Singapore doesn't require a rigid schedule. Compared to Japan, most activities in Singapore are flexible and adaptable. The MRT system is well developed, so the itinerary can be reshuffled at any point.

In terms of dining, the average cost of eating out is approximately SGD 11 (around TWD 266), roughly three times the cost in Taiwan.

Singapore struck me as a well-developed country, with green space everywhere in the city.

Alongside the Singapore Zoo there's also Bird Paradise and the Night Safari. We had planned on the Night Safari but dropped it, since the people I was traveling with would likely have been worn out by then. My past experience at a night safari in Thailand was limited visibility and poor conditions for photos, so it fell flat, and I am curious whether Singapore's does that part better.

What I remember most is the wide-open green space at the Singapore Botanic Gardens. I'll go back if I get the chance.
