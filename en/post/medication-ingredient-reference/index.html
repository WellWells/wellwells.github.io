How to read a Japanese medicine label and compare common drugstore products

Compare Japanese cold remedies, pain relievers, digestive aids, B vitamins and skin products by symptom. Use the Japanese names on the box to spot ingredients that two products have in common.

If you already have a pain reliever and are buying a cold medicine in Japan, look for 成分・分量 on both boxes. If both list アセトアミノフェン (acetaminophen), do not take them together. Too much acetaminophen can damage the liver. FDA guidance on acetaminophen

What brings you to the pharmacy?What to look for
Several cold symptomsIdentify the fever reducer, then check which cough or mucus ingredients you need.
Mainly sneezing or a runny noseCheck the antihistamine and the driving warning.
Sore throat or hoarsenessCompare oral anti-inflammatory medicines, cough granules and throat sprays.
Headache or period painLook for acetaminophen, ibuprofen and any sedating ingredients.
Indigestion, bloating or irregular stoolsLook for digestive enzymes, bacterial cultures or both.
Comparing Alinamin and Chocola BBCheck the labeled use and the daily amount of B₆.
Dry, itchy skin or diaper rashCheck the application site, age limits and directions for damaged skin.
Inflamed acne or dark spot preventionCheck whether the label says acne treatment or medicated skin care.

Check how many tablets each number covers. 1回量 means one dose, 1日量 means a daily total, and 9錠中 means the amount in nine tablets combined. The tables use Japanese formulations. Look under 用法・用量 on your own box for directions and age limits. Show the packages to a pharmacist if you are buying medicine for a child, are pregnant or breastfeeding, or already take another medicine.

Colds with several symptoms

For fever or headache alone, ask the pharmacist about a single-ingredient pain reliever. If you also have a cough, think about whether you need to cough less or make sticky mucus easier to bring up. Dihydrocodeine can cause drowsiness and can slow breathing.

B-vitamin medicines

Alinamin and Chocola BB Plus are OTC medicines in Japan. Look under 効能・効果 for the symptoms each product is labeled to relieve. Include any multivitamin when adding up your B₆ intake. Taking several B₆ products can cause peripheral neuropathy. Stop taking them and seek medical advice if you notice tingling, burning or numbness in your hands or feet. Australian TGA safety update on vitamin B₆
