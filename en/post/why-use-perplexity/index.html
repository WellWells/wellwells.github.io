What is Perplexity?

Perplexity is a free, AI-powered search engine with the following key features:

  1. Immediate search and usage without registration.
  2. Deep understanding of user queries, with answers laid out in a clear structure.
  3. Every response includes source citations, so you can check the information for accuracy and currency.
  4. Integration with multiple models such as Claude 3.7 Sonnet, GPT-4o, Gemini 2.0 Flash, and Grok-2 (the free version only offers the Auto model or advanced models with usage limits).
  5. Consolidation of diverse online resources and article content into complete answers, with sources cited.
  6. Proactive recommendations of related questions based on the search content and context to guide further exploration.
  7. Support for a Traditional Chinese interface since March (previously available only in Simplified Chinese, though responses could be in Traditional Chinese).

Perplexity aims to reshape traditional search methods, with answers that are more direct and more trustworthy. When you are not sure of the keywords yet, it works out precise terms first, then searches, consolidates, and draws the conclusion.

Reasoning Search

Perplexity integrates multiple reasoning models to make search stronger, including its optimized, unbiased DeepSeek-R1 model, OpenAI's o4-mini, and Anthropic's Claude 4.0 Sonnet Thinking model.

Searches focus on multi-layered logical reasoning, which fits complex queries that require step-by-step analysis (e.g., mathematical proofs or policy impact studies). Problems are broken into discrete steps, so responses come out more logical and more reliable.

Deep Research

Deep research was the primary reason I chose to use Perplexity. It digs deeper and wider, and covers for the times my initial keywords were incomplete or imprecise. Perplexity self-questions and refines its queries, automatically adjusting and extending keywords, so the result answers more than the literal question, though it takes longer. In many cases, I prefer using Professional or Reasoning Search to balance efficiency and quality.

Similarly, when searching for "What to do if a watermelon isn't sweet?", here is the deep research chain-of-thought (CoT):

StepReasoning
1I will perform an online search using the keyword "Watermelon Not Sweet, What to Do?" to find suggestions and solutions for handling unsweet watermelons. Through this search, I hope to uncover practical tips or recipes to improve the flavor of watermelons or make use of those that aren't sweet.
2I discovered several methods to sweeten unsweet watermelons, including adding sugar and salt. Next, I will further search using the keyword "Watermelon Add Salt Remedy" to investigate the use of salt, confirming its scientific rationale and practical effectiveness, so as to provide a more comprehensive answer to "What to do if a watermelon isn't sweet?"
3The information I found indicates that sprinkling salt can enhance the perceived sweetness of a watermelon, as salt's osmotic effect helps boost its sweetness. Next, I will use the keyword "Watermelon Add Sugar Remedy" to search for recommendations on using sugar-based seasonings to further increase the watermelon's sweetness, thereby providing additional solutions.
4I have gathered sufficient information to answer the question "What to do if a watermelon isn't sweet?" Common remedies include sprinkling sugar or salt on the watermelon, processing it into beverages or slushies, all of which can effectively improve its taste.
5It appears that the user's question is "What to do if a watermelon isn't sweet?"—that is, how to deal with an unsweet watermelon that has already been purchased. I need to compile methods from the search results on how to remedy unsweet watermelons, as well as possible tips for selecting sweet watermelons to avoid purchasing unsweet ones in the future.
6First, I will compile the remedy methods mentioned in the search results:
1. Add sugar: Sprinkle a spoonful of sugar evenly over freshly cut watermelon and refrigerate for a period before consumption.
2. Sprinkle salt: Lightly sprinkle salt on the cut surface of the watermelon; based on osmotic principles, the salt draws moisture out, reducing water content and relatively increasing sugar concentration. Additionally, salt can stimulate taste receptors to enhance perceived sweetness.
3. Add lemon juice: Drizzle some lemon juice on the watermelon to offer a refreshing taste and balance the sweetness with a touch of acidity.
4. Refrigerate: Place the watermelon in the refrigerator, as chilled watermelon tends to taste sweeter; the ideal storage temperature is around 8–10 °C.
5. Juice with seasoning: Cut the unsweet watermelon into pieces, add plain water and sugar, and blend it into watermelon juice.
6. Alcohol treatment: Cut a piece from the stem end to serve as a lid, add a few drops of brandy or white wine into the cut, then cover the watermelon and refrigerate for about one hour.
7. Make desserts: Transform the watermelon into slushies or even make sticky rice balls with watermelon.
7Additionally, I need to compile some tips for selecting sweet watermelons to help users avoid buying unsweet ones in the future:
Tips for selecting watermelons:
1. Sound test: Tap the exterior of the watermelon; if it produces a crisp sound, it indicates freshness.
2. Visual features: Choose watermelons with clearly defined stripes of varying shades on the surface.
3. Stem condition: Select watermelons with a slightly withered stem or where the stem area is sunken.
4. Surface patterns: The more distinct and clear the surface patterns, the sweeter the watermelon is likely to be.
5. Bottom size: Opt for watermelons whose bottom indent is slightly smaller than a 1-dollar coin.
6. Seed color: When buying pre-sliced watermelon, check the seed color in the flesh—the darker and more vibrant the seeds, the closer the fruit is to full ripeness.
8Additionally, here are some precautions regarding watermelon storage:
1. Once a watermelon is cut from the vine, it stops ripening.
2. Do not store it for an extended period; it is best to consume it immediately after purchase.
3. Overly cold temperatures can diminish its sweetness – uncut watermelons should be kept in a well-ventilated, cool place.
9Based on the compiled information above, I will write a comprehensive and in-depth report in Traditional Chinese to provide users with various methods for dealing with unsweet watermelons and tips for selecting them.

Conclusion

In my experience Perplexity searches better than ChatGPT. It pulls online data in real time, compares it, analyzes it, and generates follow-up suggestions, which gets past the limits of a single-model system and makes results both more accurate and more varied.

Its strength is chain-of-thought reasoning: it infers and extends from user intent, so answers land closer to what was actually being asked, and it raises possible follow-up questions on its own, which carries the conversation further. Perplexity is also not restricted to a single model architecture, and that flexibility makes it one of my first stops for daily queries.

On the last day of a trip to Okinawa, my mobile data ran out and speeds were capped at about 150 kbps, so Google Search would basically not load. Perplexity and ChatGPT still worked on a few words of input, returning answers pulled together from several webpages while using very little data.
