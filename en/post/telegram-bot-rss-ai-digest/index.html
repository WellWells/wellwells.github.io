Build an AI Telegram News Bot from RSS, With No Server and No API Key

Once your Telegram bot is connected, build the Yobi flow that watches a site every 10 minutes, summarizes each new article with AI, and pushes it to your phone. No code to write, no API key or server to keep running.

Introduction

Before push notification algorithms took over your phone, most people got their information by bookmarking a handful of sites and checking back on a schedule, or by subscribing to sites through an RSS reader. Then Google Reader shut down, and I moved to Feedly along with everyone else, and stayed there for years. Feedly later added its own AI summaries, collapsing a long article into a few sentences with one click.

But RSS is old technology by now. Once services like Facebook and Google News started pushing content at people, RSS's subscribe-and-check-back model turned passive and slow by comparison, and what shows up is more noise than anything you actually wanted to read.

So what I actually want is the reverse: new articles come to my phone, instead of me remembering to go and look. The usual answer is an automation platform like IFTTT, Zapier or n8n, but wiring in an AI summary there means adding a credit card, registering an OpenAI API key and watching metered usage afterward. For someone just starting out, that is a lot to take on.

This post takes a different route. You use a computer that is already switched on, treat an AI web interface you are already signed into as the engine, and set up a flow that checks for new articles on a schedule, writes them up and pushes them to Telegram. Every step is a mouse click, and no terminal shows up.

The tool is Yobi, the open-source desktop app I wrote (MIT licensed, Windows and macOS). I covered what it does in general in Yobi: AI Automation That Drives ChatGPT & Gemini Through Their Web Interfaces to Build Your Own Agent. This one walks the single flow people ask about most, end to end.

What you end up with

A Telegram bot of your own that checks the site you follow every 10 minutes and messages you whenever something new shows up. The template's built-in prompt turns every article into the same shape: a bold, clickable title link, two paragraphs of roughly a hundred words each, the first on what happened and the second on the hard numbers and specs, and a few hashtags. When the page yields a cover image, that text is sent as the image's caption.

You need three things: a Windows or macOS machine, a Telegram account, and one site you want to follow.

With the prerequisites out of the way, the rest takes about 5 minutes.

Prerequisites: install Yobi, connect a Telegram bot

I already covered both of these in the previous post, so just follow along there:

  • Download and install Yobi: Windows through the Microsoft Store, macOS by dragging the .dmg into Applications.
  • Controlling Yobi remotely with a Telegram or LINE bot: register a bot token with BotFather, paste it into Yobi's Bot Integrations settings, generate a pairing code and pair your account.

This is what it looks like once installed. The Chat, Flows, Logs and Settings tabs along the top are the ones this post keeps coming back to.

If you lose the token, there is no need to create another bot. Send /mybots to BotFather, pick the bot and press API Token to get it again. Once that is done, your account shows up under Paired chats and the status in the card's top right corner turns green with Running. That is everything the flow needs.

Step 1: Add an RSS digest flow

Switch to the Flows tab, hover over the New Flow button in the top left and pick Templates from the menu.

Click the first one, RSS Article Digest. The panel turns into a setup wizard with two fields to fill in.

  • Which feed to follow. Paste https://9to5mac.com/ and press "Find feed from site" underneath the field, which digs out the site's actual feed URL. If you already know it, paste it directly.
  • Who to send to. Pick your own account from the ones you paired.

The Add to my flows button in the bottom right stays greyed out until the feed URL is filled in. Press it and the flow is created, already enabled.

The same menu also has "Create with AI". Describe the automation in one sentence, such as every weekday at 8am, fetch my RSS feed, summarize each article with AI and send it to Telegram, and it lays out the whole flow for you. The template already covers what this post builds, so the template is the faster route. Save this one for combinations the gallery does not have.

Step 2: Check every 10 minutes

The trigger sits at the top of the flow. The template ships with a schedule that runs once every weekday at 8:00. To check every 10 minutes instead, switch "Weekly" to "Interval", set it to run every 10 minutes and press Save.

A tight interval does not flood you. The RSS step remembers how far it read, and articles it has already pushed are cached and not sent again, so running every 10 minutes just means new articles reach you sooner. The first run also fetches only the newest single article to establish that checkpoint, so a feed's entire back catalogue never lands on your phone at once. After that, each run sends at most 5 articles. To start over from scratch, the RSS step has a Reset Checkpoint button.

To check it works right away, press Run beside the trigger. Fetching the page and waiting for the AI to read it and write the summary takes anywhere from half a minute to a couple of minutes. There is no progress bar, so to see which step it is on, switch to the Logs tab at the top.

Yobi is a desktop app, so a schedule only fires while the computer is awake and Yobi is running. Under Settings → General, in the System Tray section, turn on "Launch at startup" and "Hide to system tray when window is closed" and it sits quietly in the background. A machine at home that never sleeps is the ideal host.

A schedule is not the only trigger. Switch the same row of tabs to Bot, give it a command name, and the flow runs once whenever you send /news to the bot on Telegram. That suits anyone who would rather ask for it than be interrupted on a timer. Whatever text follows the command lands in the variable named under Command Argument Name, and later steps can use it, for instance as a keyword inside the prompt.

Changing the format means changing the prompt

The flow runs fourteen steps, but the skeleton is "fetch the feed, open each article for its body, hand it to the AI, push it to Telegram". Everything else is a condition that skips an article that failed to fetch and moves on. Only one box decides what the message looks like.

Scroll down in the flow editor to the LLM Summarize card.

The large box in the middle is the prompt, already written for you (in English, with the output following your own language). Replace it to get a different format, for example:

1
2
3
4

Summarize this article. Format it like this:
First line: the article title in bold, linked to {{item.link}}.
Then three sentences on what the article actually says.
Last line: one sentence on whether it is worth clicking through.

Under the box, the variables available at that point are listed. {{item.title}} and {{item.link}} are this article's title and link, and clicking one inserts it. Press Save, then Run to see the message it actually sends. Nothing else breaks if you get this box wrong, so keep adjusting until it reads the way you want.

Scroll further down to the last card, Telegram Notify. The Message box holds {{llm_1}}, which is whatever the AI wrote in the step before. The Attachment box holds the cover image picked up while fetching the page, and when there is one the message becomes its caption, which is where the example earlier comes from. To send the same digest to family, click another paired account under Chat ID.

Following a site with no RSS

Plenty of sites publish no feed at all, or you only care about one page of a site, like a company's press release list. The "Custom Web Tracker" template in the same gallery covers this, and its wizard has one more field than the RSS one. Paste the URL and press "Pick list", which opens that page in a window. Click the title of any article on it, and Yobi works out where the list is and where the links are.

From then on, every run compares that page for new items and only continues when it finds some. Fetching the body, summarizing and pushing are the same as in the RSS flow, and the prompt is changed the same way.

There is a template for YouTube channels too

YouTube Video Digest in the same gallery is the video version of the same skeleton. The first step becomes YouTube Subscriptions, with one channel per line as an @handle or a channel URL, and a Skip Shorts switch that keeps only regular videos. The steps after that fetch each new video's transcript, hand it to the AI and push the result to Telegram. A newly added channel seeds only its latest video, so adding one never dumps its whole back catalogue on you.

Conclusion

Since this flow went live I have hardly opened Feedly. The sites I follow are all in there, new articles arrive on my phone by themselves, and the title plus two paragraphs is enough to decide whether to click through. To follow a different site later, open the flow and change the feed URL under Flow Settings rather than building it again.

The other templates in the gallery share the same skeleton, and a few of them together cover more involved jobs: one flow for RSS, one for YouTube, plus a bot command you can call from Telegram whenever you want.

If you come up with a combination worth sharing, or have suggestions for Yobi, open an issue on GitHub where the source lives too. If it saved you some time, you can buy me a coffee or star the project.
