Amazon US Shopping Log: Buy&Ship Forwarding & a Rare Double-Shipment Incident

Forwarding two Apple Watch Series 11 units from Amazon US with Buy&Ship: how to fill in the warehouse address, how shipping is calculated, roughly TWD 7,000 saved against Taiwan retail, and what happened when the package arrived after the warehouse had closed.

Preface

This February, during Amazon's Presidents' Day sale, the Apple Watch Series 11 dropped from USD 399 to USD 299 (USD 100 off, about 25%). In Taiwan, the same Apple Watch Series 11 GPS 42mm is TWD 13,900, so I immediately ran the numbers.

A family member's watch had a cracked screen, and I also wanted to replace mine, so I decided to buy two from Amazon US. Quick math: USD 598 total (about TWD 19,000) versus TWD 27,800 in Taiwan. The watches alone were nearly TWD 8,800 cheaper, so even after forwarding costs it still made sense.

I looked for a US-to-Taiwan forwarding service and chose Buy&Ship, a package-forwarding platform for overseas shopping. The process was mostly smooth, but one small oversight led to a double-shipment. This post records the entire journey from order to delivery.

Buy&Ship Forwarding Platform

Why Buy&Ship

Search for "US package forwarding to Taiwan" and you'll see a long list: Buy&Ship, Shipgo, ELF, EZGO, Planet Express, PAPA K... I compared a few options, and the reasons I picked Buy&Ship were pretty simple:

  • TWD 170 per pound, tax and home delivery included. Among the services that list their prices, this was one of the lowest tiers
  • 30 days of free warehouse storage: no need to worry about packages sitting too long and getting charged
  • The most thorough documentation: forwarding process, how to fill in the warehouse address, how shipping is calculated, everything had Chinese guides with screenshots

For someone like me doing this for the first time and not wanting to deal with anything overly complex, it was an easy pick.

Forwarding Process

The process itself isn't complicated: Sign up and get a warehouse address → Place your Amazon order and ship to the Buy&Ship warehouse → Declare your shipment in the Buy&Ship dashboard → Once it's checked in, request consolidated shipping → Pay and have it sent to Taiwan. Right after signing up, I got a warehouse address in Portland, Oregon (a tax-free state). Just plug that into Amazon's shipping address and you're done, five minutes tops.

After your purchase, remember to go to the Buy&Ship dashboard and "Add Shipment" to declare it: basically just filling in what you bought on Amazon and how much it's worth. When shipping to Taiwan, you can optionally add loss protection, but regardless of whether you do, the shipping fee already includes customs duties.

Buy&Ship Things to Note

A few important official rules worth reading before you order:

  • After the seller ships, declare the shipment in the dashboard ASAP. Forgetting or misreporting will delay processing.
  • If tracking shows delivered but it hasn't been checked in after 48 hours, contact customer support.
  • Overseas warehouses don't accept packages on Saturdays, Sundays, or public holidays. Deliveries get pushed to the next business day.
  • The Hong Kong warehouse offers 30 days of free storage; after that it's TWD 40 per item per day, and after 45 days, items are treated as abandoned.
  • Consolidation limits: single item max 44 lbs; consolidated shipments max 10 items / 22 lbs, no single side exceeding 80 cm, total of three sides not exceeding 150 cm.
  • Single item value cap: TWD 30,000; total per forwarding order: TWD 50,000.
  • Prohibited items: perfume, nail polish, compressed gases, masks, meat, etc. Check the prohibited items list before shopping.

Ordering & the Delivery Mishap

On February 9th, I ordered two Apple Watch Series 11 GPS 42mm units on Amazon, USD 299 each. The ordering process itself was unremarkable: I filled in the Buy&Ship warehouse address, paid, and waited for shipment.

Amazon's shipping speed was as fast as ever, it shipped the same day.

Then the problem happened.

I didn't notice that the Buy&Ship US warehouse's business hours only go until 4:00 PM (Monday to Friday, 9:00 AM to 4:00 PM, PST).

The Amazon delivery arrived at 4:13 PM local time, the warehouse was already closed. The delivery driver left the package at the warehouse door, snapped a delivery photo, and walked away.

When the delivery notification came through, there wasn't much I could do. The package was just sitting there at the door, no one watching it, no surveillance, and no one would be there to handle it until the warehouse reopened the next morning. Two Apple Watches worth nearly USD 600, left outside overnight. I was worried that if they went missing, it'd be a nightmare to deal with.

I decided to contact Amazon customer service immediately.

This next part is the most eventful part of the whole ordeal. I contacted Amazon customer service three times total, spanning three days. Here's the complete timeline:

First Contact: Package Left at the Door, CS Instantly Arranges Replacement

On the evening of 2/9, I reached out to Amazon's online customer service and explained the situation: the package arrived at 4:13 PM, but the warehouse closed at 4:00 PM, and the box was sitting at the door with nobody around.

The rep's response was swift. After confirming the situation, they straight-up said "I can request the replacement," barely asking for any additional details. I also asked if the new delivery could arrive during business hours (before 4 PM), and the rep verbally agreed.

The whole thing took less than 15 minutes, and the replacement was arranged. The rep also specifically reminded me: "Please ignore the return label."

Second Contact: Both Shipments Received, but Account Shows Return Instructions

The next day (2/10), after the Buy&Ship warehouse opened for business, both items were received and accounted for: the original package that had spent the night at the door wasn't lost and was successfully checked in.

But when I logged into my Amazon account, the first order's status had changed to "Your Return Instructions." In short, Amazon wanted me to take a QR code to a Whole Foods store and return the item by 2/25. The problem? I'm in Taiwan, and the goods are in a forwarding warehouse in the US, so there's absolutely no way I'm going to a Whole Foods.

Worried about getting charged if I didn't comply, I contacted customer service again.

The second rep handled it decisively:

"I will right away check this and disarm this return label for the original order. So that you will not be charged for this."

"Disarm" the return label: that's the key phrase. It means they cancel the return request in the system so you don't need to physically return the item and won't be charged.

Final Outcome

The end result: both the original package and the replacement safely made it into the warehouse, and Amazon didn't charge me a single extra cent.

Once the replacement also arrived at the warehouse, I went ahead and submitted a consolidation request through Buy&Ship to ship both items back to Taiwan. The two packages weighed 1.3 lbs and 1.5 lbs respectively, totaling 2.8 lbs—Apple Watch with its packaging is actually pretty light. I submitted the consolidation request on 2/21, Buy&Ship shipped via Yamato (Black Cat) home delivery, and it arrived at my doorstep on 2/27.

The whole timeline was stretched a bit because Chinese New Year fell right in the middle. From ordering on 2/9 to receiving the goods on 2/27, it was about 18 days total. For international package forwarding, that's not too bad. Without the holiday, it probably would've been a few days faster.

This double-shipment situation was honestly a stroke of luck. Amazon customer service did disarm the return label and didn't charge me after the fact. But that doesn't mean every similar situation will end this way, if the rep doesn't handle it correctly, or if the system doesn't update before the deadline, there's a real chance you'd get charged.

Total declared value was TWD 37,417. The savings on the two watches came out to nearly TWD 7,200—roughly four times the Buy&Ship shipping cost. If you're only buying one, the savings shrink a bit, but since shipping is weight-based and the per-pound rate stays the same, the shipping cost as a proportion is actually even lower. The Taiwan-bound shipment was tax-inclusive. Once it arrives, confirm the declaration in EZ WAY (Taiwan's real-name import app) and wait for home delivery.

Conclusion

Buying an Apple Watch through Amazon US with Buy&Ship forwarding genuinely saves a decent chunk of money. Buy&Ship has a friendly Chinese interface and transparent tax-inclusive pricing, so it's an easy first forwarder to use, and TWD 170 per pound works well for the occasional high-value purchase but not for low-value everyday items. Amazon's customer service was also better than I expected, resolving things quickly both times.

⚠️ The most important lesson: always check the warehouse's business hours before placing your order. Otherwise, when the delivery shows up at the door and nobody's there to receive it, the amount of follow-up work will make you want to cry. I was off by 13 minutes, and it cost me three days of back-and-forth with customer service.
