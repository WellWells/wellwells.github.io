Introduction

Since its debut at CES 2026 earlier this year, the Aqara U400 has been high on my smart home watchlist. While my current place didn't have an urgent need for a lock upgrade, my long-standing interest in Ultra-Wideband (UWB) technology—and a recent stroke of luck—finally landed a unit in my hands.

This is a straightforward unboxing, plus a look at how UWB fixes the security and precision problems of Bluetooth unlocking.

Why Do We Need UWB Technology?

Ultra-Wideband (UWB) isn't exactly a new buzzword. Apple brought it into the mainstream back in 2019 with the U1 chip in the iPhone 11. However, it's only now, in 2026, that we're seeing its killer app potential fully realized in the smart lock industry.

Solving the Bluetooth Problem

In the past, many smart locks attempted "auto-unlock" using Bluetooth, but the experience was notoriously hit-or-miss. Bluetooth relies on Received Signal Strength Indicator (RSSI) to guess distance, which is easily interfered with by walls or the human body. This led to awkward scenarios: sometimes the door would unlock while you were still waiting for the elevator; other times, you'd be standing right in front of the door with zero response.

UWB uses Time of Flight (ToF), which works like radar, to calculate the exact time it takes for a signal to travel back and forth. This allows the lock to know your absolute distance and whether you are actually approaching the door. This precision is what makes hands-free unlocking finally feel reliable.

Beyond accuracy, UWB offers two major advantages in security and spatial awareness:

  • Relay Attack Prevention: UWB uses STS (Scrambled Timestamp Sequence) technology. Even if someone tries to intercept and relay your signal to spoof your location, faking the timing is close to impossible, which is what matters for residential security.
  • Indoor vs. Outdoor Detection: This is perhaps the most practical feature. By combining UWB with Angle of Arrival (AoA) algorithms, the lock doesn't just know how far away you are; it knows your direction. It can distinguish between you walking around your living room and you approaching from the outside, effectively ending the annoyance of the door accidentally unlocking while you're just walking past it inside.

Note: We are starting to see products supporting Bluetooth 6.0 Channel Sounding in 2026. While this tech also aims to improve ranging, UWB currently maintains a clear lead in positioning accuracy, interference resistance, and overall ecosystem maturity.

Prerequisites and Limitations

Adopting new tech does come with a few caveats:

  • Heavy Apple Ecosystem Reliance: Currently, the hands-free experience is essentially tailor-made for Apple users. You'll need an iPhone 11 or later, or an Apple Watch Series 6 or later. Crucially, you need a Thread-enabled hub (like a HomePod or Apple TV) before the response feels instant.
  • Double-Door Configurations (Taiwan Context): Many apartments in Taiwan feature a "foyer gate + main inner door" setup. If you install the U400 on the outer gate, I highly recommend enabling "Dual Door Mode" in the Aqara Home App and refining the "Approach Direction" settings. This prevents the lock from constantly cycling if you're just pausing in the foyer to change shoes.

Hardware Unboxing & Specs

Packaging and Accessories

The Aqara U400 box highlights support for multiple protocols, so it works with almost every major hub on the market.

Key features on the box include:

  • Matter: This is what gives it cross-platform compatibility. Whether you use Apple Home, Google Home, Amazon Alexa, Samsung SmartThings, or Home Assistant, it connects directly without being locked into one ecosystem.
  • Thread: Compared to traditional Bluetooth or Wi-Fi, Thread offers faster response times, lower power consumption, and a more stable mesh network for local automation.

Inside the box, you'll find the interior and exterior assemblies, latch hardware, mounting screws, a USB-C rechargeable lithium battery, an M100 Thread hub (included in some kits), and a paper template for precise installation.

Lock Body Durability and Power Management Design

The image below clearly shows the internal structure of the Aqara U400's front and rear panels. The exterior panel on the left features a connecting cable and the spindle, the core mechanical component that drives the rotation of the latch. Meanwhile, the interior panel on the right houses the batteries, supplying power to the exterior side via a ribbon cable.

During actual installation, the spindle passes through the square hole of the latch, and screws are then used to tightly secure both the interior and exterior panels to the door. When the motor activates or the knob is manually turned, the spindle engages the latch to complete the locking and unlocking actions.

Installation & Setup Tips

While the U400 is marketed as a sandwich-style installation that fits standard deadbolt holes without extra drilling, every door is different. Check your specs before you remove your old lock. Also, if your old hardware has been there for years, a can of WD-40 helps with the seized screws.

Door Measurements

Before starting, verify these dimensions:

  • Door Thickness: Ideally between 35mm and 55mm.
  • Bore Hole Diameter: If your current hole is only 38mm, the official recommendation is to expand it to 54mm so the wiring and the install have room.
  • Handing: The U400 is universal (supports left/right and inward/outward swings).
  • Backset: The distance from the door edge to the center of the handle hole. Use the included paper template to verify this instantly.

Critical Step: Latch Calibration

Once the hardware is powered up, the most important part of the app setup is Calibration. The motor will extend and retract the deadbolt automatically. This isn't just a test; it's the system learning the physical limits of your door frame:

  1. Finding the Limits: The system needs to know exactly how deep the bolt goes to be "Locked" and how far it must retract to be "Unlocked." This minimizes mechanical wear and keeps the motor from overworking itself.
  2. Auto-Lock Reliability: Without accurate calibration, the auto-lock might fail to engage fully or, conversely, exert too much pressure on the frame, leading to battery drain or false alarms.

Software Integration

For this initial test, I used the included Aqara M100 gateway as the Thread Border Router. If you're setting this up permanently, a HomePod or Apple TV is what gets you the instant response time.

Out of the box, my unit was on version 3.1.0.0, and the Home app immediately prompted a firmware update. Do the update. It includes the groundwork for Aliro, the upcoming cross-platform UWB standard that will eventually allow Android phones to enjoy the same hands-free experience currently exclusive to Apple.

Multiple Ways to Unlock

The Aqara U400 offers 9 different ways to get inside. This makes it friendly for everyone from tech-enthusiasts to grandparents who prefer traditional methods.

Two standout software features:

  • Phantom Passwords: To stop onlookers from reading your code, you can type up to 20 digits of random numbers, as long as your actual 6-digit code is hidden somewhere within that string.
  • Scheduled Access: Useful for cleaning services or temporary guests. You can issue "One-time passwords" or codes that only work during specific windows (e.g., Wednesdays from 2 PM to 4 PM), so access rights can be sliced quite finely.

Is it worth it for Android users?

To be objective: Android users currently miss out on UWB hands-free unlocking. The fingerprint and NFC experience, though, is already solid. Since the hardware already includes the UWB chip and Matter over Thread, the parts for what comes next are already in the box. Once the Aliro protocol is fully integrated into the Android ecosystem, a simple software update should unlock the full feature set.

Conclusion

The Aqara U400 does more than get rid of your keys. With UWB added, it can tell that you are walking toward the door rather than just standing near it.

While the Android ecosystem still needs time for UWB and Aliro support to fully mature, if you're already an Apple user, this lock has no real competitor right now.

Comparing it to established brands in the Taiwan market—such as Samsung, Philips, or Yale—you'd typically need a budget of over TWD 20,000 to find hardware with similar specs, and even then, they often lack UWB or Apple Home Key support. By contrast, the U400 is currently available on Amazon for roughly TWD 8,500. Even factoring in international shipping, the total cost stays well under the TWD 10,000 mark.

If you're in the market for a new lock and want to cover everything in one go, the Aqara U400 is worth buying now.
