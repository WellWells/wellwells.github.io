Best AI Subscription 2026 Is Claude, Not ChatGPT or Gemini

Perplexity cut Pro search to 200 queries a week. Copilot moved to token billing. I tested ChatGPT, Gemini, and Antigravity, then consolidated on Claude.

Introduction

Over the past year the AI tools have been in a brutal fight, with someone shipping a stronger, cheaper option every few months and knocking the last one down. Look back through this blog and you'll see I backed whichever gave the best value at the time:

  • 2025/03: I championed "Perplexity In-Depth Review: Differences from ChatGPT, Feature Comparison, and Daily Use", using it to replace my daily Google search, and paid for a full year on the spot.
  • 2025/04: I made GitHub Copilot's agent mode my main coding driver in "Using GitHub Copilot's Agent Mode to Boost Coding Efficiency".
  • 2025/11: I cancelled ChatGPT and moved wholesale to Gemini in "2025 AI Subscription Reshuffle: Maximizing Value with GitHub Copilot and Gemini 3", with family sharing at roughly TWD 110 per person.
  • 2025/12: I finished a from-scratch project in a single week with Google Antigravity in "Antigravity vs. GitHub Copilot: Features, Performance, and Cost".
  • 2026/04: After Copilot moved to token billing and got pricier, I published "GitHub Copilot Moves to AI Credits Billing: 2026 Plans and Alternatives".

Over the past year, the tools I recommended raised prices, cut quotas, got flaky, one after another, and the value kept sliding. My subscription list only got shorter, until one thing was left: Claude. This post traces my own usage trail, spelling out how each tool's value collapsed, and where my money goes now to get the most out of it.

When this was verified: every price, quota, and policy here was re-checked in early July 2026. The AI industry moves fast, so the numbers may already be different by the time you read this. Always defer to each vendor's latest announcement.

Perplexity Quietly Shrank Its Paid Quotas

Perplexity once all but replaced my Google search: answers came with cited sources, travel lookups were fast, and back in March 2025 I paid for a full year.

The problem is that in early 2026 it quietly trimmed the paid quotas, without emailing anyone or grandfathering existing subscribers. Per tech-press reports, from around February, Deep Research dropped from hundreds of runs a day to only 20 a month, advanced-model queries went from a clear daily allowance to a vague weekly cap that support couldn't put a number on, and file uploads went from unlimited to a weekly cap. A second round in May tightened it again, with many users hitting the weekly wall after only a few queries. Every feature that got cut is advertised as unlimited on Max, so it degrades Pro and then nudges you toward the USD 200/month, USD 2,000/year Max plan.

Perplexity doesn't tell you how much you have left, but there's actually a rate-limit API you can check once logged in. Checking it, the paid Pro plan now allows only 200 Pro searches a week, and once they're gone you wait until the next week for them to reset. And when the quota runs out it doesn't block you or warn you, it just quietly downgrades the model to the weaker Sonar in the background, and the drop in answer quality is obvious the moment you use it.

On Reddit and Trustpilot, plenty of annual subscribers call it a bait-and-switch, and some complain Deep Research cites sources that flat-out don't exist. For me, a research tool that won't even tell me how many runs I have left this week is hard to keep as a main driver. For web search I now lean more on Gemini's Deep Research or Claude. My Perplexity subscription hasn't lapsed yet, so I'm still using it for now, but I doubt I'll renew next time.

ChatGPT Is Stable but Now Just My Backup

ChatGPT was the first thing I subscribed to, though not the one I kept longest. It was always billed monthly with no lock-in, so the moment Gemini rolled out its annual discount, I switched over right away. Back then the biggest complaint was how bad its Chinese image generation was, and Gemini's Chinese output was noticeably more accurate once it arrived. Translation, photo recognition, and web summarization all just work, and day to day it has no real problems. Just one thing stops me from keeping it as my main tool.

It occasionally trips the content moderation. Perfectly ordinary requests sometimes get a "can't do that", not every day, but when it happens it blocks you. Since the switch from 4o to GPT-5 it's also picked up some new quirks: emoji show up constantly, the tone of its replies feels different, and it reaches for an "I can't do that" line far more often, which makes it feel a lot more restricted. ChatGPT later launched a cheaper Go tier, but the trade-off is ads, which drew even more criticism.

For coding, I was mainly on Copilot back then, comparing GPT-4o against Claude Sonnet: Claude was noticeably stronger, so my impression of GPT ended up being mostly about its chat behavior. Codex 5.3 later jumped up a lot in capability, but Claude kept rolling out Sonnet, Opus, and Fable, different models for different needs, so Codex still isn't my first choice for coding, and day to day I still mainly use Sonnet 4.6. As for everyday chat, search, and summarization, Gemini and Claude are level with it, so I have no reason to keep a separate ChatGPT, and it has slipped into backup duty.

Gemini Family Sharing Is Still the Best Value

Gemini is one of the few that didn't raise prices on me this year, and it's something I use almost daily. Google AI Pro is TWD 650/month in Taiwan, one subscription can be shared across up to six people, and it also throws in YouTube Premium Lite and 5 TB of cloud storage (originally 2 TB, now bumped to 5 TB for free), so a full group averages about TWD 110 per person, and Deep Research, NotebookLM, and the 1M long context are all things I still use. The value really is high. Paying for it also grants a higher agent-model access allowance inside Google Antigravity, Google's agentic development platform. To save even more, some resellers still offer Gemini AI Pro group-buy slots on personal accounts before 2026/7/30, in groups of six at about TWD 166 per month total for the whole group, which is about the cheapest way in on the market right now.

There are downsides. Gemini Apps Activity is on by default, meaning your conversations are used to train models by default, with a subset sent for human review, and paying doesn't auto-disable it. It's not a big deal for me. If you mind, just turn off Gemini Apps Activity or use a temporary chat. The model naming is also a bit of a mess: the personal paid tier gives you three options, 3.1 Flash-lite, 3.5 Flash, and 3.1 Pro, while the free tier only gets 3.5 Flash. With version numbers jumping around like that, I couldn't really tell you which one is actually better, but for everyday chat I mostly just stick with 3.5 Flash.

Research and long-form summarization nearly all go to Gemini now, and nothing else comes close at this price.

GitHub Copilot Kept Cutting Models and Billing

Copilot was the first tool I used for coding, and the one I felt most keenly about. It just changed really fast. It started out as a Microsoft product with a free education tier that ran for years, and it only moved to token billing after enough people abused that. When VS Code first shipped agent mode in April 2025 it was genuinely good, editing across files right in the editor, and it was my main driver for a while.

Then GitHub spent the year cutting. First the student and education plans lost the ability to manually pick high-end models, then Sonnet got cut, then GPT-5.3 Codex, until the only thing left to use freely was a GPT-4o-class model. What used to be unlimited runs of 4o and mini turned into per-token billing. On June 1, 2026 it moved fully to AI Credits (1 AI Credit = USD 0.01), and agent mode burns through them fast.

Copilot doesn't build its own models, it runs on Claude and GPT underneath, so it's really just passing along the token costs Anthropic and OpenAI charge it. Cursor is in the same boat, and Perplexity is too in its own way. Most people using these for coding or research are actually running Claude behind the scenes most of the time. Wrapper tools like this are only going to keep getting pricier, and once you're paying something like TWD 3,000 a month for the wrapper, why wouldn't I just subscribe to Claude directly?

What I really couldn't stand is the shared enterprise pool. The whole team's AI Credits are pooled, so you're effectively spending other people's AI Credits. On our four-person team, one colleague had zero sense of usage at all and just asked away freely, burning through about TWD 500 a day just running Opus 4.7, which drained all four of our allowances in the first week and left the rest of us unable to use it normally, and I got cut off after asking just two questions that week. That design is genuinely bad.

The heavy lifting, agents and cross-file refactors, I moved wholesale to Claude Code, where the same budget buys more usable capacity, and a few days in it's far less stressful. Copilot in VS Code I've now just switched off entirely.

Antigravity Is Too Unstable for Real Work

In December 2025 I opened Google Antigravity. The single-week from-scratch project mentioned above was built with it, and I posted about that right away. It's Google's agent-first, VS Code-based IDE.

But half a year of use made the problems clear, and the worst is that the quota just isn't enough. A few queries in and I'm already throwing server errors with the current allowance drained, and now and then a task dies halfway through with the failed run still eating quota, which is maddening.

Privacy is the same old issue: the public preview has no zero-retention guarantee, the code you send can be used for training and seen by human reviewers, and there's no clear in-product switch to stop it. It's not that I'm scared to put anything confidential through it, it's just that I wouldn't really recommend using it for work. Most of the time I use it for other side projects, or to fill in once I've burned through my Copilot quota. It also went fully agent-driven with the 2.0 release, stripping out a lot of what VS Code gave you out of the box, like the SSH remote development I lean on, and I stopped following its updates, so I'm not sure it's back.

I respect its agent orchestration, but a tool whose quota drains this fast, and that can lock your account at random, can't carry real work, so I treat it as a second IDE for experimental projects.

Why I Consolidated Coding on Claude

Claude is close to first in almost every category now. The parts I actually live in are the CLI and the MCP integration, with Skills and plugins hooking in on top. Image and audio generation are the weak spots, but it can still lay out a PowerPoint deck or export a full PDF. What I care about is stability, value for money, and completeness, and by value for money I don't mean cheap, I mean getting the most out of what you spend. Prices and quotas side by side first:

For coding, Claude is already the only choice for me. After Copilot moved to AI Credits, cost became unpredictable and the team pool drained in a week, so I just switched all the heavy lifting, agents and cross-file refactors, over to Claude Code. The biggest difference is predictable cost: it shares the same subscription allowance as Claude chat, so I roughly know how much I can run in a day and don't break into a cold sweat before every run. On real cross-file refactors and debugging, it swallows an entire large file and explains as it edits. Anthropic's latest Opus 4.8 hits 88.6% on SWE-bench Verified, among the best for coding right now. But the benchmark only gets you so far. What actually separates these models in practice is tool calling and image reading: knowing when to reach for cmd or PowerShell to check system state or find files, and reading a screenshot or an error dialog correctly before acting on it.

More importantly, one subscription covers two needs. I used to run Gemini for chat and Copilot for coding, two subscriptions and two bills. Claude puts everyday chat and Claude Code coding into the same allowance, far cleaner for a heavy user and simpler to account for.

There's also a telling detail: even when I'm inside Antigravity or Copilot, the model I actually reach for to do the heavy lifting is often still Claude. Antigravity's settings let you swap the default Gemini for Claude Sonnet 4.6 or Opus, and Copilot's model picker has long included Claude. These tools are raising the price of the shell, but the engine everyone inside keeps reaching for is often Claude, so I'd rather subscribe to the source.

Consistency, too. On writing quality the community broadly rates Claude above GPT and Gemini, and finds it less likely to drift on long documents and long-running agent tasks. That "I can hand this off and relax" feeling is the main reason it's my primary tool.

Claude's Downsides and Limits

Claude still falls over when it's busy. Anthropic's demand exploded this year and the infrastructure is visibly strained, 529 overload errors and outages crop up periodically, and in June 2026 the press flagged a tenth disruption in twelve days, which I occasionally hit when I'm busy. Limits also bite heavy users. There was a March wave of complaints about Claude Code draining unusually fast (later traced to a caching bug that Anthropic reset), and Max at USD 200/month is genuinely pricey, so running agents all day still hits a wall. And don't take the long context at face value: it advertises 1M tokens, but in practice the middle gets overlooked, so don't dump everything in just because the window is long.

I believe the service will only keep getting better. What worries me is today's token pricing creeping up further, or quotas getting cut again, which would just repeat the same pattern as the other tools above, pushing everyone toward pricier plans.

For cheap research, Gemini family sharing wins, and Copilot still has the fastest inline completions. Claude can't beat those two for now. But when it comes to coding, Claude currently has no rival.

Conclusion

The biggest change this year is that everyone, in unison, moved from hooking you with low or free pricing to quietly charging you real cost. Perplexity cutting quotas, Copilot cutting its models to the bone and switching to token billing, Antigravity tightening the free cap, are all fundamentally the same thing. The cash-burning honeymoon is over, and token costs have quietly been climbing the whole time.

If you want the money to actually work, here's what I'd do:

  • To code well: I'd suggest starting with the cheapest plan for a month to get a feel for it, then deciding whether to move up. If you go for Claude Max's entry-level USD 100 (~TWD 3,200) tier, you could also split it with 2-3 people, that's enough capacity to run agents and refactors. I'm a heavier user myself, so I'm actually on the USD 200/month tier.
  • Just research and everyday chat: Claude Pro annual at USD 17/month (~TWD 550) is enough, and it handles search and summarization fine.
  • To save more: Gemini family sharing at about TWD 110 per person still covers research, you just have to mind the privacy yourself.

My answer right now is to consolidate on Claude, but I know full well this is only the best option in mid-2026. If Claude also starts raising prices, cutting quotas, and getting flaky, I'll drop it without hesitation and come back to write a post eating my own words.
