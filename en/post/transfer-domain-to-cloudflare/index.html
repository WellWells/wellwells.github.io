Transferring a Domain from Squarespace to Cloudflare: A Complete Walkthrough

Background

This is a straightforward record of my experience transferring a domain from Squarespace to Cloudflare.

The process itself isn't particularly complicated, but there are some waiting periods and details to keep in mind along the way. I figured I'd write it up for anyone in the same boat.

My domain was originally registered with Google Domains. In 2023, Google sold the entire Google Domains business to Squarespace, and all registrations were automatically migrated over—no choice in the matter. At the time, Squarespace promised to honor Google's original pricing for at least 12 months, which sounded reasonable enough.

Once the grace period ended, though, renewal prices jumped. For my .com domain, the estimated 2026 renewal fee increased by a whopping 23%:

ItemIDRApprox. TWD
2025 (paid)184,182.30~371
2026 (estimated)245,000.00~457

Going from TWD 371 to TWD 457 just to keep a domain registered? Not worth it. Cloudflare Registrar advertises at-cost pricing with zero markup. A quick check showed roughly USD 10 total (including ICANN fees, about TWD 325), cheaper than Squarespace's 2026 estimate and even less than what I paid in 2025.

Since my DNS was already hosted on Cloudflare anyway, moving the domain registration there too just made sense. Everything under one roof, less to manage. No reason to hesitate.

Before You Start

Transfer Costs

Per ICANN rules, a domain transfer counts as a one-year renewal. The fee you pay at transfer is effectively next year's registration fee, not a processing charge. Any remaining time on your current registration carries over. It doesn't get swallowed up.

Cloudflare's USD 10.46 breaks down transparently:

  • Wholesale domain cost: The registry fee paid to the TLD operator
  • ICANN fee: A fixed regulatory charge (~USD 0.18)
  • Cloudflare markup: USD 0

Cloudflare Registrar has followed a zero-markup model since day one. Whether it's the first year or any subsequent renewal, the price stays at cost. That's unlike many traditional registrars, which lure you in with a dirt-cheap first year and quietly raise the price on renewal. Cloudflare treats domain registration as an entry point to its broader ecosystem (CDN, security, Workers, etc.) and doesn't profit from this segment.

Compare that to staying on Squarespace at roughly TWD 457 (~USD 14.5) per year, and the savings are obvious. Plus, no worrying about another surprise price hike down the road.

Pre-Transfer Checklist

  1. Disable Domain Lock on your current registrar
  2. WHOIS privacy may need to be turned off (some registrars require this; Cloudflare handles it automatically during transfer)
  3. You have full administrative access to the domain
  4. You have a Cloudflare account, and the domain is already added to Cloudflare for DNS management

💡 If your domain isn't on Cloudflare yet, I'd recommend adding the site to Cloudflare Dashboard and pointing your DNS there first. Registrar transfer and DNS hosting are two separate things, but getting DNS sorted beforehand makes the rest of the process much smoother.

Transfer Timeline

The entire process took about 5–6 days, and the website experienced zero downtime. Here's the timeline so you know what to expect:

Day 1, February 17, 2026: Transfer initiated

  • 20:00 – Submitted contact info change and requested removal of the 60-day transfer lock on Squarespace.
  • 23:00 – Received the domain transfer authorization code (auth-code) from Squarespace.
  • 23:00 – Cloudflare confirmed the transfer process had officially started, generated an invoice, and submitted the request to the current registrar.
  • 23:00 – Squarespace sent a transfer confirmation email stating the transfer would auto-approve by 2/22 unless manually cancelled.

Day 2, February 18, 2026: Routine reminder

  • 15:00 – Received a Cloudflare notification reminding that the domain was nearing expiration.

Day 6, February 23, 2026: Transfer complete

  • 13:00 – Squarespace auto-approved the transfer request. Transfer completed successfully.
  • 13:00 – Cloudflare confirmed the domain had been transferred in, with a request to verify the email address on file.

After logging back into Squarespace to confirm the domain was gone, I deleted my Squarespace account entirely.

Step-by-Step Transfer Process

Step 1: Unlock the Domain and Get an Auth Code from Squarespace

First, head to Squarespace and turn off Domain Lock.

After logging in, go to Domains → select your domain → Overview, then toggle Domain Lock to Off.

A few things to note in the screenshot:

  • RENEWS ON: Renewal date and amount (mine was March 3, 2026, IDR 245,000)
  • WHOIS PRIVACY: On (private registration)
  • DOMAIN LOCK: Set to Off

Once unlocked, scroll to the bottom and click REQUEST TRANSFER CODE to get the authorization code (also known as an EPP code).

⚠️ The auth code has an expiration window. Use it promptly after receiving it.

One thing to be aware of: after clicking REQUEST TRANSFER CODE, the code won't appear on the page itself. It gets emailed to the admin address. In my case, it took about 2.5 hours to arrive.

Step 2: Initiate the Transfer on Cloudflare

With the auth code in hand, log into Cloudflare Dashboard and navigate to your domain's page. You'll see a prompt along the lines of "Transfer your domain registration to reduce renewal costs" with a button to start the transfer. Click it.

This takes you to the Transfer Domains page. Just follow the guided flow.

Step 3: Enter the Authorization Code on Cloudflare

Paste the auth code you received from Squarespace into the Authorization code field.

Key details on this page:

  • Domain name: wellstsai.com
  • Auth code verification: A green checkmark means validation passed
  • Transfer cost: USD 10.46. Future renewals will stay at this price
  • The page warns "Domain is within 30 days of expiration," meaning transferring too close to the expiry date carries risk. Best to start early.

Once everything looks good, click Continue to payment.

Step 4: Complete Payment

Next, fill in your personal details and payment method. Cloudflare accepts credit cards and PayPal.

Submit the form, and Cloudflare will begin processing the transfer request.

Step 5: Wait for the Transfer

After submitting, Cloudflare displays a status of "Waiting for your previous registrar to release the domain."

The page mentions you can go back to your old registrar and manually "approve" to speed things up, but Squarespace doesn't offer a manual release or approve option. So it's just a waiting game—up to 7 days max.

During this period, you can click View transfer status to check progress anytime.

Tracking Transfer Progress

Post-Transfer Notes

A few things to keep in mind after the transfer:

  1. DNS records are unaffected: If you were already using Cloudflare DNS, transferring the registrar doesn't touch any DNS records. Your site stays up.
  2. Auto-renew: Double-check that auto-renew is enabled after the transfer completes.
  3. WHOIS privacy: Cloudflare includes WHOIS privacy protection for free. No extra configuration needed.
  4. 60-day lock: Per ICANN rules, the domain cannot be transferred out again for 60 days after a transfer-in.

Conclusion

Looking back, the whole process is really not that complicated. The core flow is: unlock → get auth code → paste it in Cloudflare → pay → wait. The most time-consuming part is just waiting for Squarespace to release the domain.

From Google Domains being acquired, to getting involuntarily migrated to Squarespace, to leaving Squarespace over price hikes and landing at Cloudflare—it's been quite the journey. But I finally ended up somewhere with fair pricing and a clean interface.

Having DNS and domain management all in one place is so much more convenient. Should've done this ages ago.
