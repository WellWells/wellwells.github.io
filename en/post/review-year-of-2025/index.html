Career Transition

In "2024 Year-End Review", I mentioned my dissatisfaction with the work environment and management system, and at that time, I chose to adapt and adjust myself. However, this year I made a more decisive decision: to leave that suffocating environment and stop wasting my life with incompetent management.

Looking back, the bad management and pay made one thing clear: staying in a dead-end environment wasn't worth it. Time to find something actually worth working toward.

The article "Stepping Out of the Comfort Zone to Confront Anxiety" documents my process of facing self-doubt and anxiety during the job hunt. Hard as it was, it left me more certain that the energy belonged back on myself.

The biggest takeaways this year:

  • Refuse Internal Friction: Stop consuming emotions and energy on people and things that aren't worth it.
  • Take the Lead: Stop waiting for the future to arrive. Start steering toward it.
  • Believe in Yourself: Going from resigning without a job to being re-employed proved my value.

Looking back, it was the right call. No more playing along with people who don't deserve the time, and my energy goes into things that actually matter.

Personal Outlook

Last year's technical goal was to migrate from Node.js, MongoDB, and Elasticsearch to Deno, PostgreSQL, and Next.js. Complete the full-stack integration and build out a whole system.

But I hit quite a few pitfalls in technology selection during actual development. These experiences have been summarized in "Mastering Agentic Workflow: A Practical Guide to AI-Driven Project Development". The originally planned full-stack system had too many pieces to maintain and my motivation drained away, so I now lean toward finishing small projects and stacking up momentum one Micro-win at a time:

  • Infinite Taiwan: An infinite synthesis game centered on local elements like bubble tea, TSMC, and night markets, using the Vite + Preact + PWA tech stack.
  • Taiwan DNS Query Tool: A DNS RPZ blocking check tool, integrating Cloudflare Workers and D1 database to achieve a serverless architecture with zero maintenance costs.
  • GemTube: A browser extension optimizing the interaction experience between Gemini and YouTube, already listed on Chrome and Edge stores.

What I've Been Up To

TV Shows

I've been keeping up with Netflix's Stranger Things. The fifth and final season is set to drop on January 1, 2025, at 9 AM Taiwan time. I'm really looking forward to seeing how this beloved series wraps up after all these years.

I've also been watching Pluribus on Apple TV+, which premiered on November 7, 2025 with its first two episodes. The premise is quite unique: one day, over 7.3 billion people suddenly "join" a collective consciousness from outer space. They share thoughts, cannot lie, and pursue absolute peace and non-violence. The protagonist, Carol, is one of only a dozen or so "survivors" worldwide who weren't assimilated. She must find answers in a world that seems utopian but has lost all individuality. The atmosphere is built around a "quiet apocalypse": no explosions, yet filled with the fear and sorrow of individuals being absorbed by the collective. The story concept is fascinating, and I highly recommend giving it a watch. A second season has already been ordered.

The show's marketing was also quite clever: the trailers featured a phone number "(202) 808-3981", and calling it would play the following message:

Hi, Carol.

We're so glad you called.

We can't wait for you to join us.

Dial "zero" and we'll get back to you via text message.

Subsequent text messages included trailer alerts and event invitations, with all recipients addressed as "Carol". I happened to have a US phone number and tried texting it. It really made me feel like I had become Carol from the show 😂.

Outlook for 2026

This year gave me a more grounded view of technology selection. My priorities for the coming year:

  • Continuous Growth at Work: Currently, work is gradually producing results. Although limited by hardware architecture, I will focus more on development and discussion at the algorithm and system levels.
  • Refining Advanced Projects: On the basis of existing technology, challenge projects with more depth.
  • Focus on Delivery: Instead of setting too many goals, focus on a few items that can actually be completed.

AI tools have moved fast: from copy-pasting code into a chat window, to calling tools and running multi-step Agent tasks; the Plan–Execute loop has gotten more mature and transparent; and the Skill mechanism is finally landing in real workflows, pushing things toward modular and reusable.

The most important lesson 2025 taught me: Done is better than perfect, and shipping is more valuable than planning.
