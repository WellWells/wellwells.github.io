A Taobao mahjong table refused by official consolidation reached Taiwan by duty-included sea freight: weight limits and volumetric pricing.

Introduction

An automatic folding mahjong table costs CNY 1,694 on Taobao. With the conversion to Taiwan's voltage, the total stayed under CNY 2,000, or less than TWD 9,000. I had bought small things from Taobao before, including cables and a bathroom mirror, and had even been through a return. Those orders were simple: enter the address of Taobao's official consolidation warehouse at checkout, and the platform handles everything else.

The mahjong table was different. The seller put it plainly: "We don't ship to Taiwan." The manufacturer pays to deliver the goods to a consolidation warehouse inside China, and the seller takes no responsibility once they leave the country. The table shipped on August 24, and on the morning of August 26 I learned that the official consolidation warehouse had refused it.

This post follows the table from checkout to my door in Taiwan, with every cost along the way. The last sections cover the customs rules I only understood afterward and how to choose a third-party consolidator.

Ordering: specs and the seller's seven warnings

Specs agreed in the chat window

I bought a folding table from the best-selling brand on Taobao. This is the full spec line from my order notes:

Crated, voltage converted to 110V, quiet four-player machine, faux jade N40-144, gray, G Legend, inner cover panel, folding gray legs, four-player spare parts kit, delivered to ground floor, self-assembly

The product page offers only a few options, so almost every spec that matters has to be agreed with customer service in chat.

  • Voltage and plug. The factory model runs on China's 220V/50Hz. For an extra CNY 300, the seller converts it to 110V and notes the change on the order. The plug can't be changed. The seller only has Chinese three-pin cords, and when I asked for a two-pin version, the answer was no. The machine takes the same connector as a desktop computer's power cord, so buying a Taiwanese cord is easier than using an adapter.
  • Tile size. The seller's chart starts at No. 38 (about 3.7 cm) and No. 40 (about 3.9 cm), adding 0.2 cm per size up to a custom No. 60 at 5.9 cm. To find your size, lay ten tiles end to end and measure the total length. My current set measured 39.5 cm, or 3.95 cm per tile, which matches No. 40. Customer service said buyers in Taiwan usually choose 40, 42 or 44.
  • Tile count. Sets range from 72 to 152 tiles. A 136-tile set has the winds, dragons and three suits, and a 144-tile set adds the eight flower and season tiles. Taiwanese sixteen-tile mahjong uses the flowers, so ask for 144, because the 136-tile sets common in China leave them out. An automatic table comes with two sets.
  • Wooden crate. The listed price is CNY 150, for the main unit only. Customer service offered to crate it for free on the spot.
  • Heater and wireless features. My table has no heater, Bluetooth or remote control. When Taijiyun quoted the shipment later, their customer service asked about each of these.

Bulky goods this size must be crated, so don't skip the crate to save the fee. A mahjong table is one large tabletop with a lifting and tile-shuffling mechanism underneath. Between the domestic courier, the consolidation warehouse, the sea voyage and delivery in Taiwan, it gets handled many times, and cardboard alone won't hold up.

The seller's seven warnings and how I answered

The seller's customer service sent me the same checklist twice, once before I ordered and once after. Six of the seven points concern overseas shipping:

  1. Will your consolidation warehouse accept a mahjong table? One tracking number covers several boxes, the main unit weighs up to about 45 kg, and with the cover panel it comes to five boxes. If the warehouse refuses it, the buyer pays shipping both ways.
  2. Does the warehouse have size or weight limits? Mahjong tables count as bulky goods and can't be sent by air.
  3. Do you need a wooden crate, and will the warehouse accept one? The crate covers the main unit only and costs CNY 150.
  4. The machine runs on China's 220V, 50Hz.
  5. The plug is the Chinese three-pin type. Check that it works for you.
  6. The warehouse entry fee is CNY 150, and free shipping only covers mainland China. If the courier doesn't collect the fee, it's refunded the following month.
  7. Overseas orders get no on-site after-sales service, replacement parts ship only within China, and you assemble the table yourself.

The first two points were exactly where things went wrong. To the first, I replied that I was using Taobao's official consolidation, so it wouldn't be a problem. That only confirmed which service I was using. The second asked about size and weight limits, and I said the table was going by sea, which didn't answer the question. Both were questions for the consolidator, and I answered them myself. The first point also says the buyer pays shipping both ways after a refusal. I avoided that because the address on a Taobao order can still be changed while the goods are in transit. The destination was the only thing left to change, and I paid a CNY 120 redirect fee instead.

Why official consolidation refused it

August 26: a single verbal reason

On the morning of August 26, the seller's customer service forwarded a message from the courier:

The courier says the consolidation warehouse refused it. They don't accept mahjong tables.

At 10:17 the same day, an exception appeared in the JD Logistics tracking:

Consumer phone number incorrect, unable to contact

That evening, the seller's after-sales team also asked whether the recipient was not answering the phone. Both messages probably describe the same event. After the refusal, the courier needed to call me, but the phone number on my Taobao order is Taiwanese, and a courier in Xiamen can't dial it. The only reason I ever got for the refusal was that one line, relayed verbally by the courier. Nothing was put in writing, and I still don't know for sure why it happened.

After the refusal, I searched online and found a 70 kg limit. I assumed the shipment had exceeded it, told the seller so, and didn't look any further at the time.

What I later learned about 70 kg, customs and delivery limits

Later I checked Taobao's published list of consolidators and shipping limits. For home delivery in Taiwan, the weight limit per package is between 20 and 30 kg.

Convenience store pickup is even stricter, with a limit of 5 to 10 kg. When I compared this with the Customs Administration's rules, I realized a bulky shipment from Taobao to Taiwan can get stuck in three places, each governed by a different party. The 70 kg limit I had found is only one of them:

  1. Whether the consolidator will take it. Each consolidator decides which product categories and features it accepts. The courier's message that they don't accept mahjong tables is this kind of refusal. It reflects the consolidator's own policy and has nothing to do with import bans in the tariff schedule.
  2. How it clears customs. Under the Regulations Governing Customs Clearance for Sea Express Consignments, only goods of 70 kg or less gross per piece can clear through the sea express zone. Anything heavier has to be filed on a general import declaration, which is what people mean by a formal declaration. Gross weight includes the crate and the cardboard.
  3. Whether the last leg can deliver it. That's the table above.

My shipment got stuck at the first and last of these. The crated main unit measured 105 × 105 × 45 cm and weighed 58 kg, almost double the most generous limit. Its dimensions add up to 255 cm, which only SF Express allows. After the redirect to Taijiyun, the warehouse weighed the heaviest box at 53.26 kg, still far over the home delivery limit. By that table, none of the boxes could have been delivered to my door. Customs clearance was never the issue. All four boxes were under 70 kg, so the limit I thought I had hit had nothing to do with this shipment.

Goods over 70 kg can't clear through the sea express zone. They have to be filed on a general import declaration, a formal process with far more paperwork. For a shipment that heavy, send a third-party consolidator the gross weight and outer dimensions of each box and ask them to assess it, since the consolidator may be able to handle the formal declaration for you. When you ask customer service about weight limits, first make clear whether you mean what the consolidator accepts, customs clearance or last-leg delivery.

Redirecting to Taijiyun after dispatch

The address can only change once

After the refusal, I changed the delivery address to the Zhangzhou warehouse of Taijiyun (台集運), a third-party consolidator.

The next morning, the seller's customer service opened with: "Dear, the address can only be changed once." That is this seller's own rule, not a Taobao policy, and other sellers may handle it differently. Once an order has shipped, whether you can change the address depends on the seller being willing to contact the courier for you. If you're switching warehouses, decide once and don't count on a second chance.

A consolidation warehouse address also includes your member ID, which the warehouse uses to match packages to your account. Don't leave it out when you copy the address.

A CNY 120 redirect fee, known only afterward

The seller's customer service said a new address meant sending the package again at my expense, and that the fee wouldn't be visible until the redirect was done:

The address change fee can only be seen after the redirect succeeds

The courier finished rerouting the package on the morning of August 27. At noon on August 28, customer service quoted CNY 126, then corrected it to CNY 120 that afternoon, and I paid through a price-difference link the seller sent. This wasn't a new shipment. The package already in transit was redirected, and the tracking showed a single line saying it would go to the new address, with no mention of a fee.

The CNY 100 entry fee I didn't pay

That same afternoon, the seller's customer service relayed another charge from the courier's support team: a CNY 100 warehouse entry fee. They asked me to pay CNY 100 for entry plus CNY 120 for the redirect.

Point six on the seller's checklist does mention a CNY 150 warehouse entry fee. That fee is part of the free domestic shipping the seller covers, though, and it's refunded the following month if the courier doesn't collect it.

Instead of paying, I asked Taijiyun's customer service. They said the warehouse charges no entry fee. Other customers had reported couriers adding one, but the warehouse needs nothing from the courier beyond leaving the goods in the receiving area, and there's no queue. Their advice was to ask the seller to use a different courier, since some don't charge this fee.

I sent a screenshot of that conversation to the seller's customer service, and only then did they say they would check with the courier. A cross-border order puts the seller, the courier and the warehouse between you and your goods, so confirm any fee relayed by one of them with the party said to be charging it. Saving CNY 100 took me a few minutes.

Signed for at the warehouse, missing from my account

Under the delivery entry, JD's tracking adds a note: once the domestic courier marks a package as delivered, the consolidation warehouse still has to confirm receipt, and if that hasn't happened within 48 hours, you need to contact the courier or customer service yourself. That's exactly where my shipment stalled. The tracking number showed the boxes delivered and signed for at the Zhangzhou warehouse, but my Taijiyun account was empty. Customer service needed the tracking number before they could assign the shipment to my account. After that, the boxes appeared with the status "pending details."

An official consolidation address contains an identifier, so a package is matched to its order as soon as it arrives. My shipment had been redirected halfway, and the warehouse simply received four unclaimed boxes. The original tracking number for official consolidation was void by then, and only the seller and the courier had the new number created by the redirect. Taijiyun could assign the boxes to my account only after I got that number from the seller's customer service.

If a package is redirected mid-route, ask the seller for the final tracking number. The seller can look it up but won't offer it, because as far as Taobao is concerned, the order has already shipped.

How Taijiyun quotes sea freight

What customer service asked for

Taijiyun's customer service has two tiers. Regular agents handle the first contact, and anything beyond what sea express can take goes to a sea freight specialist. I opened with just "Can you ship a mahjong table? 110 kg." What followed was a round of questions: product photos or the Taobao link, whether it had Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, a remote, a heater or a battery, what it was made of, and my full delivery address in Taiwan. A product name isn't enough to tell whether something can be shipped. The features determine how the goods are classified, and the full address shows whether a remote-area surcharge applies.

Once they had all that, the quote came back as a single line:

Mahjong table J, Zhangzhou/Dongguan warehouse. Sea freight: CNY 6/kg OR CNY 36 per volumetric unit (duty included, damage not covered)

The rates are the same at the Zhangzhou and Dongguan warehouses.

Sea express or sea freight

The two services quoted very different prices for the same shipment:

Customer service based both quotes on the figures I gave them: 105 × 105 × 45 cm and 110 kg. Sea express has a per-package weight limit, and after seeing my weight, they recommended sea freight right away. At that weight, sea freight was also a third cheaper. Sea freight includes import duty by default. I never asked whether sea express does.

I also tried Taijiyun's online calculator, which gave me CNY 945. When I sent customer service a screenshot, they told me sea freight isn't in the system, the online price for it isn't accurate, and the price a specialist calculates by hand is the one that counts. You also can't switch a bulky order to sea freight on the website yourself. In customer service's words, "We place sea freight orders for you on the back end." Quotes, item names, the order itself and payment are all handled through customer service.

Why 84.3 kg was billed as 17 volumetric units

Taijiyun's terms state the formula:

Billing is based on the greater of volumetric and actual weight. Volumetric formula: length × width × height ÷ 28316 = units. Anything under 1 kg or 1 unit is billed as 1 kg or 1 unit.

Dimensions are in centimeters, and one unit is about 0.028 m³.

Before I changed the address, the seller sent estimated sizes and weights. The folding-leg model ships in four boxes:

  • Main unit with crate: 105 × 105 × 45 cm, about 58 kg
  • Base: 50 × 32 × 54 cm, about 10 kg
  • Frame: 33 × 22 × 102 cm, about 12 kg
  • Tiles: 39 × 32 × 19 cm, about 15 kg
  • Cover panel: 97.5 × 97.5 × 10 cm, packed inside the main unit
  • Full set: about 110 kg

The total of 16.68 volumetric units rounds up to 17, and 17 × CNY 36 is CNY 612. The actual weight of 84.3 kg rounds up to 85 kg, and 85 × CNY 6 is CNY 510. Customer service had made this clear with the very first quote:

These prices are for reference only. The final charge depends on the dimensions and weight the warehouse measures when your packages arrive. If the weight, dimensions, material or features differ, the quote and the final shipping cost will change.

The fine print on duty-included sea freight

Crates and packaging

Sea freight has its own rule for wooden crates:

Wooden crates for sea freight cannot be raw wood and must be fumigated or painted. If we handle it, the fee is CNY 20 per package.

If the seller's free crate is untreated wood, it has to be fumigated or painted before it can go by sea, and Taijiyun charges CNY 20 per box to do that. The fee didn't appear on the final quote for my four boxes, but ask the seller what wood they use before they build a crate.

The same terms cover damage claims:

Sea freight does not cover damage. If packages arrive at the warehouse without packaging or without proper padding and reinforcement, damage in Taiwan cannot be claimed. Please make sure the seller reinforces the packaging inside and out.

Before I placed the order, customer service reminded me again that fragile items aren't covered for damage, and asked for the seller to pack against impact and crushing before dispatch.

Fees, responsibilities and delivery rules

The sea freight price includes import duty, and I didn't need to hire a trucking company to collect the goods from the port. Duty included doesn't mean the recipient can ignore customs entirely, though. These are the terms that matter most for bulky goods, and my shipment's size and weight stayed below every surcharge threshold among them:

  • Minimum charge of CNY 300. Anything less is billed at CNY 300, and that doesn't include remote-area, oversize, overweight, stair carry, crate removal and disposal, fumigation or forklift fees.
  • Maximum package size of 300 × 180 × 180 cm. Going over on any side adds an oversize fee.
  • A CNY 100 forklift fee for any package over 150 kg. You also need your own unloading equipment in Taiwan.
  • You supply the customs information. That means the item name, origin, photos, purpose, quantity, material, brand and model, plus a catalog or manual for machinery. If the details don't match and customs holds or seizes the shipment or imposes a fine, the customer bears full responsibility.
  • Boxes are opened for inspection before dispatch. Customs sampling of up to 5 items counts as normal procedure.

Delivery rules are in a separate notice:

  • Furniture containers arrive about 5 to 10 business days after loading, and general cargo containers take 10 to 15, depending on customs clearance. If you need delivery by a certain date, tell customer service before ordering, because asking to postpone after arrival leads to storage charges.
  • For packages over 70 kg, you need to provide people to help unload, and anything over 300 kg generally requires your own forklift. This 70 kg is an unloading threshold and has nothing to do with the 70 kg customs limit.
  • Delivery is to the ground floor or a parking area only. Drivers don't bring goods indoors, upstairs or into basements. If a large truck can't reach the address, the handover takes place at the nearest point it can reach, and carrying anything upstairs is negotiated with the driver at your own cost.
  • After customs clearance, they call to schedule delivery. If they can't reach you before the free storage period ends, you pay the storage fee, and once the truck has left, the address can't be changed and delivery can't be canceled.
  • Deliveries run Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 18:00. You can't choose a delivery time unless you pay for a dedicated vehicle. For my shipment, the Taiwanese delivery company called the day before to set a time around 7:00 a.m., and the driver arrived at 6:30 a.m.

Twelve days from payment to delivery

Sea freight isn't in Taijiyun's system, so payment happens through a separate Taobao order. Customer service sends a price-difference link, you pay and return the order number, and they create the consolidation order. In my case they did it on the spot and sent the order number and delivery details for me to double-check.

Customer service also set the customs description by hand: the four boxes were declared together as "mahjong components," with a declared value of CNY 1,694.

The boxes arrived at the warehouse and I paid for sea freight on August 28. The driver reached my home at 6:30 a.m. on September 9, 12 days after payment.

I wasn't the declarant, and EZ WAY, Taiwan's customs verification app, never sent a single notification. With US package forwarding, you verify your identity in the app yourself. When a consolidator files under its own name, the case never reaches the recipient's app, so getting no notification is normal.

After delivery: the crate, assembly and power

Only my parents are home during the day on weekdays, so they signed for the delivery and took the crate apart. One adult and one child removed it with a hammer, so this step was easy.

Assembly takes real effort. The main unit is too heavy for one person to move. Be careful with the instructions from after-sales support, too. When I asked about assembly, they sent several different versions, and some were for other models. Give customer service your model number before you start and ask them to confirm the instructions match, or you'll waste a lot of time just identifying the right one.

Customer service also gave me three warnings without being asked. Remove the tiles before folding the table. Never wash the tiles, because water ruins the magnets. Keep the boxes for a while. The tiles contain magnets the machine uses to sense which way each tile faces, and faux jade tiles feel like stone, so it's easy to assume you can rinse them. The boxes are for returns or replacement parts, if you ever need them.

Once assembled, the converted 110V table ran straight from a Taiwanese outlet without a transformer. For power, I bought a 3 m AC cord with an IEC C13 connector and a US-style plug for TWD 150.

It has worked fine so far. I told customer service I didn't want ashtrays, and the table came with cup holders and ashtrays anyway. I use the recess for my phone. The one thing to get used to is that the folded table has nowhere to store tiles, so both sets need their own bag.

Formal declaration cases from a consolidation group

My shipment never needed a formal declaration. The cases below come from members of a consolidation group. Besides the per-piece gross weight limit above, a customs value over TWD 50,000 or an item subject to import restrictions can also move a package from a simplified express declaration to a formal one. According to the Customs Administration, the recipient then has to sign a power of attorney and provide ID, and the consolidator no longer covers the duty.

Weight is counted per piece, and it also matters which weight the warehouse records. One member's shipping record showed 61.6 kg, but they received a formal declaration notice because the warehouse had calculated 70.07 kg from the box dimensions. They asked the customs broker to measure again, and two days later the package was back on simplified clearance. In another case, customs asked for more documents after the switch to a formal declaration. The consolidator did nothing, and the buyer couldn't see the case in EZ WAY or update anything themselves.

Taiwan bans mainland Chinese goods unless they have been explicitly approved for import. Anything marked MW0 in the import regulations column of the tariff schedule can't be imported, so a shipment within every weight limit can still be stopped because of what it contains.

How to choose a third-party consolidator

After official consolidation refused my table, I changed the address to Taijiyun. What I know about choosing a consolidator came later, from reading back through the group.

Disputes in the group

The disputes I saw in the group all stemmed from how the price was estimated. The consolidator gave a low estimate before the goods arrived, then billed by volume once they were in the warehouse. One member's CNY 1,520 estimate became a CNY 6,390 bill, and another went from CNY 190 to CNY 1,350. By then the goods are in the consolidator's warehouse, and withdrawing them or moving them to another company adds outbound, storage, forklift and customs fees, one after another.

Official consolidation won't take bulky furniture, so a third-party consolidator is the only option left. Someone using one for the first time has no idea what a fair price looks like, and the only question they know to ask is the price per kg. Ads focus on that number too. One consolidator advertised on Facebook at TWD 10/kg, stressing that the price was in TWD and not CNY, with free crating and guaranteed compensation. One member who had a dispute found their consolidator through a furniture installer on Threads and never compared prices at all.

The member with the CNY 1,520 estimate posted the rate sheet: CNY 8.5/kg for sea express, and CNY 2.5/kg or CNY 15 per volumetric unit for furniture by sea. Those are normal rates, and nothing on the sheet looks wrong. The problem was the estimate given before the goods arrived. The consolidator calculated CNY 1,520 from weight alone, then billed by volume.

What to give a consolidator and what to ask

A third-party consolidator has two rates, one per kg and one per volumetric unit, and bills whichever gives the higher total. Furniture is large for its weight, so the volumetric rate almost always wins, and the price per kg alone tells you little about the final bill. My shipment weighed 84.3 kg and measured 16.68 volumetric units. By weight that's CNY 510 and by volume CNY 612, and I was billed CNY 612.

When you ask for a quote, give the gross weight of each box including packaging, plus its outer dimensions: four boxes, four sets of numbers. Then ask for a written total based on those figures. If you can't get one, don't send your goods to that warehouse. On delivery day, measure the boxes yourself and record a video. One group member used that kind of video to get an overcharge refunded.

Ask all of these before your goods reach the warehouse. Once they're inside, moving them elsewhere over an answer you don't like means paying an outbound fee:

How to check a consolidator

Before you order, search the company name, the warehouse address, the website and the person in charge. That tells you more than any ad. A group member compiled more than a dozen consolidator names that all use the same warehouse address: 廣東省東莞市洪梅鎮洪金路1600號 in Hongmei, Dongguan. Hongjin Road runs through a logistics park, so a shared address doesn't mean a shared company. Still, when a search for the name finds nothing, a search for the address sometimes does.

Search a consolidator even when someone you know recommends it. With a consolidator you haven't used before, send something small first, check the delivery time and the bill, and only then trust them with furniture.

Screenshot every conversation, from the first quote to the final charge. A screenshot is how I avoided paying the CNY 100 entry fee. When one group member's goods were held, they called 12345 to complain to the local market regulation bureau about price fraud and wrongful detention, and recovered goods that had been held for a month. Whether a complaint is accepted still depends on the evidence you have.

What I paid compared with the going rate

While writing this post, I looked through recent quotes in the same group. For an 81-unit sofa, another consolidator quoted CNY 15 per volumetric unit or CNY 3/kg, duty included. My rates of CNY 36 per unit and CNY 6/kg were exactly double. Rates do vary by category, and a mahjong table counts as machinery while a sofa is plain furniture, but a gap that large is a good reason to get more quotes before ordering. In the same thread, someone was quoted TWD 7,590 for home delivery or TWD 4,600 to collect from the Kaohsiung warehouse. If you have a vehicle, collecting it yourself saves almost TWD 3,000. Another person in that thread paid over CNY 2,000 for sea freight and was still waiting after 100 days.

Conclusion

Bulky furniture on Taobao is cheap, and nearly all the trouble with this mahjong table came from shipping it. Official consolidation refused the table, and even if it had accepted it, each box was too heavy for home delivery. I only started looking for another warehouse after the first one rejected it.

To ship something bulky from Taobao to Taiwan, go to a trustworthy third-party consolidator from the start. Before the seller ships, confirm the gross weight and outer dimensions of each box, ask the consolidator for a written estimate based on those figures, and ask whether hidden fees such as a warehouse entry fee or a forklift fee apply.
